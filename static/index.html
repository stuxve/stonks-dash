Your target, stop and trailing stop are always yours to type; everything else is a suggestion you can click. Exits are saved on each buy in the asset's own currency.

  • Resistance / support chips come from daily closes of the last 3 years (bad ticks removed): turning points found with 5-, 10- and 20-day look-backs, kept only if at least two agree, grouped into zones as wide as the asset's typical daily move and ranked by touches (recent ones count more). 3× = touched three times. ★ = within 3% of the analyst consensus. A stop chip sits half a daily move under a support.
  • Analysts: Yahoo's mean consensus target for stocks. For ETFs an implied target (≈, dashed): the consensus upside of each top holding weighted by its share of the fund, with how much of the fund that covers (funds without holdings data borrow them from an ETF on the same index). Crypto has no analyst coverage. "likely to break" when the target is more than 5% above the nearest resistance; "★ agrees" when a resistance is within 3% of it.
  • Trailing stop: either a fixed % or × ATR (chandelier stop): highest close since the buy minus k × the typical daily move (22-day average of close-to-close changes), so volatile assets get room and calm ones a tight stop. 3 is a common k. The highest of your fixed stop and the trailing stop is used.
  • Chance: probability of the price touching the level within the horizon, from the asset's own volatility, assuming no trend. Not a forecast — a way to compare how far a level really is.
  • Broke out: the price closed above your target, or above a resistance it was under in the last 4 weeks, for 2 days by more than a daily move. Raise stop moves the stop just under that level and the target to the next resistance, instead of selling.
  • P/L at target is after the account's sell fee (Costs tab) and before tax. Nothing is sold automatically. In Spain sales are matched oldest buy first (FIFO), so a buy flagged FIFO 2/3 can't be sold before the older ones.

Risk figures replay today's holdings and weights over past prices (in your base currency), so they describe the portfolio you have now. Volatility and drawdowns of the past don't cap future ones. Holdings with a short price history (e.g. a listing Yahoo barely covers) borrow the earlier part from a proxy: another listing of the same fund, an ETF on the same index, or a ticker you choose. Stocks listed after a crisis are estimated from their beta × the index (crypto: BTC). Earnings dates come from Yahoo; an estimated window means the company hasn't confirmed the day. A stock often moves several % the session after it reports — a stop inside that range can be triggered by the report alone.

Focus

A screen, not a recommendation: it ranks stocks on today's data and on how they fit your portfolio. It can't see the future, news or anything that isn't in the numbers. Use it to find names worth researching, then read the company's reports before buying.

Each factor is a 0–100 percentile inside the list (valuation is compared within the same sector when there are 4+ peers). The score is the weighted average of the factors you choose. Stocks with missing data are scored only on the factors they have, with a small penalty.

  • Fit is the personal part: it rises for sectors and regions where your stocks are below a global index, for low correlation with your current holdings (last year of daily returns, in your base currency), and falls for companies you already own, directly or through your funds.
  • Income uses the yield after the dividend withholding you can't credit in your Spanish tax return (the part above the usual 15% treaty rate: e.g. Germany 26.4%, Switzerland 35%, France 25%). Spanish dividends have no foreign withholding. Some excess can be reclaimed from the other country with paperwork.
  • Company data (Yahoo: valuation, margins, growth, dividends, analyst consensus) is refreshed daily; prices, momentum and fit update with the page.

    The whole path to your goal with the same goal, monthly amount and orders per month as above. It is recalculated every time you record a buy.

    The projection simulates 2,000 possible futures month by month: each month's return is random around the expected return, with the volatility of your mix (calculated from its last 3 years of prices; type your own to override) and realistic fat tails. It shows the median path, where most paths land, and the chance of reaching your value — an assumption-based range, not a prediction.
